# APL and the ORN→KC remapping of odor–odor correlations

Nov 3, 2026 · @Tom

## Bottom line

APL output accounts for roughly a quarter to a third of the pair-specific ORN→KC reorganization in these two matrices; most of it survives APL block. Blocking APL output doubles the mean KC pair correlation (0.16 → 0.34) but leaves the rank order of pair distances largely intact (Spearman 0.87 across the 120 pairs). Rank agreement with the ORN matrix rises only from 0.66 to 0.75 (16 odors), closing about 28% of the gap to a rank-preserving feedforward model (\~0.98), and the same pairs deviate from the ORN prediction with and without APL (Spearman 0.82 between the two residual vectors).

Where APL does act pair-specifically, it pushes in the direction of the wild-type remapping (Spearman 0.39 with the WT KC−ORN residuals) and toward natural-source structure: alignment with VCF distances drops from 0.55 to 0.42 when APL is blocked, and the APL-blocked soma geometry becomes claw-like (Spearman with KC-claw distances 0.68 → 0.85). So APL is a real contributor, but it is the amplifier of the last stage rather than the source of the structure.

Two cautions shape the ranking below. First, a single monotone (sparsening) transform explains 94% of the control-vs-TNT differences, so most of what looks pair-specific in a linear comparison is non-specific sparsening; what survives is mainly an odor-level effect (broad, strong activators 2h, IaA, pa, eb are decorrelated extra; 1-8ol, euc, B-myr, t2h are spared) plus one block effect (fruity esters {2h, IaA, pa} pushed away from small esters {2-but, eb, ep}). Second, these are two cross-fly mean matrices with no per-fly spread, so residuals of \~0.03 are near the likely noise floor.

Most likely mechanisms, in order: (1) KC spike-threshold sparsening acting on connectome-structured input with heterogeneous KCs; (2) APL feedback whose gain tracks total PN drive and acts partly locally in the calyx; (3) presynaptic shaping of PN boutons in the calyx; (4) APL-independent KC–KC lateral inhibition; (5) structured centrifugal input to the calyx (LH, MB-C1). Measurement confounds (response density, indicator nonlinearity) need to be excluded before any of these is quantified. For the model, the pair of matrices is the most useful new constraint: one parameter set should reproduce both, with only APL output toggled.

## What the two matrices say

Blocking APL output raises every pair's correlation by a similar amount; the pair-specific remainder is small and mostly explained by one monotone transform. The control (TNT3c) matrix has mean correlation distance 0.84 (mean r = 0.16); the APL-blocked (TNT) matrix 0.66 (r = 0.34). That doubling of mean correlation matches the response-rate doubling Lin et al. (2014) reported for APL block, and the direction of the two files is consistent with the labels (the matrix with the denser, more correlated code is the TNT one).

&#91;embedded content: TNT3c\_dist\_apl16.csv and TNT\_dist\_apl16.csv (cross-fly mean 1 − r, 16 odors, 120 pairs)\]

Across the 120 pairs, control vs TNT: Spearman 0.87, Pearson 0.92. A linear fit explains 85% of the variance in control distances (residual SD 0.052); an isotonic (any monotone) fit explains 94% (residual SD 0.033). Sparse thresholded codes compress low correlations more than high ones, so within-family pairs (1-5ol–1-6ol, eb–ep, 6al–t2h, -aPine–euc) look "spared" in a linear comparison but sit exactly on the monotone curve. The control matrix also reproduces Remy's wild-type megamat KC matrix on the 66 shared pairs (Spearman 0.85), so the control cohort is representative; the TNT matrix matches that wild-type matrix equally well (0.87), i.e. the KC-specific rank structure is still largely there without APL.

What survives the monotone transform is mostly odor-level, not pair-level. Per odor, the extra decorrelation APL adds tracks total ORN drive (Spearman 0.50 with mean ORN ΔF/F across glomeruli): eb, IaA, pa and 2h get the most, 1-8ol, euc, 1-5ol and t2h the least. One genuine block effect stands out: fruity esters {2h, IaA, pa} are pushed away from small esters/ketone {2-but, eb, ep} beyond any monotone trend, while alcohol–aldehyde and aldehyde–terpene pairs are slightly spared.

The fruity×small-ester block differs from the other between-group pairs (Mann–Whitney p ≈ 1e-6), but its nine pairs share six odors, so treat it as one observation, not nine. Coarse clustering is unchanged by APL block: the same six groups (fruity esters; small esters; alcohols + aldehydes; terpenes; ms; paa) appear in the ORN, control and TNT matrices, and the one KC-specific regrouping, B-myr joining the terpenes rather than paa, is present with and without APL.

Limits of this analysis: both matrices are cross-fly means with no per-fly spread, so a residual of \~0.03 is at the likely noise floor; the two cohorts differ in genotype and rearing temperature; without the response rates I cannot separate correlation changes from density changes (denser TNT responses also admit more cells into the "responsive" set used for the RDMs); and the four validation2 odors were matched to the ORN panel at the concentrations in consensus\_mean.csv (paa −4, -aPine −1.5, euc −1.75, B-myr −2), which may not be what the APL experiment used.

## How much of the ORN→KC remapping is APL?

Removing APL output moves the KC geometry only part of the way back toward the ORN geometry, and the part that moves is the part that matters least for the natural-source story. On the 12 odors shared with the megamat panel (66 pairs), I compared both new matrices against the lab's existing distance tables (ORN terminals, PN boutons, KC claws, Remy's wild-type KC soma, the two paper models, ECFP chemistry, and the hyperbolic VCF embedding used in the thesis, averaged over its 10 seeds).

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ORN terminals | 0.78 | 0.85 | 0.88 |
| PN boutons | 0.73 | 0.79 | 0.86 |
| KC claws | 0.68 | 0.85 | 0.82 |
| WT KC soma | 0.85 | 0.87 | 1.00 |
| Uniform model | 0.78 | 0.84 | 0.85 |
| Hemibrain (wd20) model | 0.76 | 0.83 | 0.84 |
| Chemical structure (ECFP) | 0.62 | 0.70 | 0.70 |
| Natural sources (VCF, hyperbolic) | 0.55 | 0.42 | 0.45 |

Values are Spearman rank correlations between pair-distance vectors. Bootstrapping pairs (5,000 draws, 90% CI) gives TNT − control differences of +0.07 \[−0.01, +0.16\] against ORNs, +0.17 \[+0.07, +0.27\] against KC claws, −0.13 \[−0.24, −0.03\] against VCF, and +0.09 \[−0.01, +0.19\] against chemistry. With all 16 odors (120 pairs, ORN matrix built from the lab's consensus glomerular means, which reproduce the tidy-table ORN distances at Spearman 0.99), rank agreement with ORNs goes from 0.66 (control) to 0.75 (TNT), +0.09 \[+0.03, +0.16\].

Three ways of scoring APL's share give similar answers. The gap between the control's ORN agreement and the \~0.98 a rank-preserving feedforward model produces is closed by 28% (16 odors) to 34% (12 odors) when APL is blocked. The variance of the pair residuals around a linear ORN prediction falls by 18% (16 odors) to 30% (12 odors), and the residual vectors with and without APL correlate at 0.82, so the same pairs deviate in the same direction either way. For natural-source alignment, the KC−ORN residual correlates with VCF distance at 0.44 in the control, 0.43 in wild type and 0.36 with APL blocked, so the "corr minus ORN vs VCF" effect is mostly APL-independent as well.

What APL does contribute is in the right direction. Its pair-specific component (control minus the linear prediction from TNT) correlates with the wild-type KC−ORN residuals at 0.39 (p = 0.001) and with VCF distance at 0.28 (p = 0.02), and not with chemical distance (−0.03). The clearest single case is the separation of the fruity esters {2h, IaA, pa} from the small esters {2-but, eb, ep}: these two groups are fairly close in ORN space (mean 1 − r = 0.51) and in the APL-blocked KCs (0.60) but far apart in the control KCs (0.88). Conversely, the APL-blocked soma geometry is as claw-like as the wild-type soma or more (0.85 vs 0.82), so APL is the main thing that makes the soma differ from the claws on this panel.

What APL does not do: the B-myr regrouping with the terpenes, the divergence of paa from B-myr and of the small esters from the aldehydes (ep–6al, eb–t2h, ep–t2h), and the relative convergence of alcohols, aldehydes and terpenes within their families are all present with APL output blocked. The APL-independent part of the claw→soma step still raises VCF alignment (claws 0.30 → TNT soma 0.42), although those are different flies and indicators, so that number is suggestive only.

Two model observations fall out of the same table. The hemibrain (wd20) model's deviations from the ORN prediction have the right size (SD 0.114 vs 0.117 in wild type) and correlate with the measured deviations at 0.60, whereas the uniform model's deviations are tiny (SD 0.044) and correlate at 0.44; the hemibrain model's overall Spearman with the data is not better because its misses are as large as its hits. And the chapter-2 draft figure 7 (claw clustering resembles ORNs early in the response and KC somata late) points the same way as the TNT data: the remapping is built during the response by feedback, and APL is the obvious but not the only candidate for that feedback.

## Candidate mechanisms, ranked

The ranking weighs three things: what the TNT pair shows (section above), what the thesis stage-by-stage data show (the step from KC claws to KC somata carries the largest change, PN boutons a small one), and what is established about the circuit. Shares are rough; only APL's is measured.

1. **KC spike-threshold sparsening acting on structured input, with heterogeneous KCs** (amplifier + substrate). Most likely. A sparse thresholded code compresses low input correlations far more than high ones, which is exactly the monotone transform that explains 94% of the control-vs-TNT difference, and it operates whether or not APL is present. The amplifier cannot change rank order by itself, so the substrate must carry pair-specific structure: the hemibrain (wd20) model produces deviations from the ORN prediction of the right size and with 0.60 rank agreement with the data, which says the connectome already holds much of it, while heterogeneity the model lacks (claw number per KC, which sets selectivity and overlap in [Ahmed et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10529417/); subtype-specific excitability and inhibition in [Inada et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2017.06.039); food-PN convergence onto α/β and α′/β′ KCs in [Zheng et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9413950)) would decide which KCs cross threshold for which odors.
2. **APL feedback with drive-dependent gain and partly local action in the calyx.** Measured at roughly a quarter to a third of the pair-specific remapping, in the right direction. Its odor-level signature here (extra decorrelation of the strongest, broadest activators) is what a feedback whose gain tracks total PN input should do ([Prisco et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.74172): APL response scales with PN input and is regionalized around it). Its one clear block effect (fruity vs small esters, groups that overlap partially in ORN space) is what localized inhibition predicts: odors that share glomerular input inhibit each other's KCs more than odors that share none, and [Amin et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.56954) showed APL activity and its effect on KCs are spatially restricted, with self-inhibition stronger than lateral inhibition. α′/β′ KCs recruit APL most ([Inada et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2017.06.039)), so subtype weighting is part of this mechanism, not a separate one.
3. **Presynaptic shaping of PN boutons in the calyx.** Medium. The thesis finds PN boutons already shifted toward natural-source structure (ρ = 0.18 vs 0.09 in ORNs) and reorganized relative to PN dendrites. APL synapses onto boutons as well as claws ([Prisco et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.74172)), so part of this is inside mechanism 2 and is TNT-sensitive; the rest (other calyx inputs, bouton-to-bouton heterogeneity within a PN) is not.
4. **Slow, within-response dynamics: adaptation, synaptic depression at PN→KC, APL build-up.** Medium as a modulator. The draft figure 7 (claw clusters ORN-like early, soma-like late) and the nuclear-GCaMP soma readout, which integrates the late phase, mean that any slow feedback or depression process shows up as "soma-specific" remapping. This is the time axis of mechanisms 1–3 rather than a separate cause, but it determines which model readout window should be compared with the data.
5. **APL-independent KC–KC lateral inhibition.** Low to medium. KCs have abundant axo-axonic connections acting through mAChR-B that suppress odor-evoked calcium in neighbouring KCs ([Manoim et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9613607/)), and the hemibrain data in the repo include KC-claw→KC-claw synapses that the model does not use. These would survive APL>TNT and could carry part of the residual structure, but their effect on soma spiking rather than axonal calcium is not established.
6. **Structured centrifugal input to the calyx (LH centrifugal neurons, MB-C1, aminergic neurons).** Low to medium, speculative but attractive. Odor-category tuning in the lateral horn is stereotyped and innate, and the thesis already notes that PN over-convergence onto KCs resembles PN input to LHNs; a GABAergic LH→calyx pathway would inject category structure directly into KC input and would be APL-independent. Nothing in the present data tests it.
7. **Measurement and analysis confounds.** Not a mechanism, but able to inflate apparent pair-specificity: odors with more responsive KCs get inflated Pearson correlations with everything, the "responsive cell" selection used for RDMs depends on density, nuclear GCaMP6s saturates, and claws and somata were imaged with different indicators. These must be ruled out before the shares above are taken literally.
8. **Experience-dependent plasticity of PN→KC or APL→KC synapses.** Low. Invariance of KC geometry across flies argues for genetically specified or shared-experience structure; it cannot be tested in silico with current data.

## In silico experiments (olfsysm + al\_analysis)

The single most informative experiment is a joint fit to both matrices: one parameter set must reproduce the control matrix with APL on and the TNT matrix with APL output set to zero. Everything else below is a way of localizing what fails when it does not. All experiments share one harness.

**Harness.** Input: the 16 APL-panel odors, built by concatenating `megamat_orn_deltas()` and the four validation2 columns (paa, -aPine, euc, B-myr) from `validation2_orn_deltas()` at the concentrations actually used in the APL experiment. Base model: the chapter-3 claw model (`one_row_per_claw=True, prat_claws=True, pn_claw_to_apl=True, claw_dynamics=True, use_connectome_APL_weights=True`, `target_sparsity` 0.05 or 0.10, `n_spikes_for_response=2`), with the paper wd20 model as the simple comparator. Readout matched to the lab pipeline: binarized `responses` and `spike_counts`, silent model KCs dropped, Pearson across KCs, 1 − r. Score every run with the same statistics used in this doc, so the targets are explicit: mean r 0.16 (APL on) vs 0.34 (APL off); Spearman 0.87 and isotonic R² 0.94 between the two model matrices; Spearman with ORNs 0.66 → 0.75; fruity×small-ester block residual about +0.05; residual-vs-VCF correlation 0.44 → 0.36. Sweeps that only change thresholds, APL scales or coupling should use `make_fixed_param_kc_simulator` rather than re-tuning.

1. **E1, APL knockout, two flavours (mechanism 2 vs 1).** Acute: tune normally, then zero `rv.kc.wAPLKC` (and `wAPLPN` in bouton models) and call `run_KC_sims(mp, rv, regen=False)`, or pass the tuned `fixed_thr` with `wAPLKC=0.0`; thresholds stay fixed, mimicking TNT. Homeostatic: `target_sparsity_factor_pre_APL=1.0` with zero APL, so thresholds alone set the rate (three days at 30 °C may have allowed some homeostasis in the real flies, so both are worth having). Readout: compare model APL-on vs APL-off to control vs TNT. If the model reproduces the mean shift and the 0.87 rank agreement but its two matrices relate by a perfect monotone map (isotonic R² ≈ 1, no fruity×small-ester block), the data's block implicates local or heterogeneous APL (E2, E5). If the APL-off model matches the TNT matrix (Spearman > 0.85) the feedforward substrate is adequate and the problem is the APL stage; if it does not, the substrate is wrong (E4, E5).
2. **E2, local APL (mechanism 2).** Sweep `apl_unit_coupling` over {0, 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10, `max_apl_unit_coupling()`} in `apl_coupling_sweep.py` matched mode, so response counts are equal across couplings. Readout: block residual and residual-vs-VCF as functions of coupling. Prediction: weaker coupling increases separation of odor groups that share glomerular input (fruity vs small esters) more than groups that share none (alcohols vs terpenes), up to the point where self-inhibition dominates and decorrelation falls (Amin et al. 2020).
3. **E3, drive-dependent gain (mechanisms 2 and 3).** Data target: APL's extra decorrelation per odor tracks ORN drive (Spearman 0.50). Vary how much PN drive is flattened before it reaches APL: expose `mp.pn.inhsc`, `mp.pn.inhadd` and the `mp.ln` parameters in `fit_mb_model` (currently not set there), or pre-scale `orn_deltas` columns in Python; toggle `pn_claw_to_apl`; sweep `wAPLPN`×`wPNAPL` with `step_model_pn_apl.py` in bouton models. Readout: per-odor APL effect vs ORN drive, and whether the strongest odors (eb, IaA, pa, 2h) are the ones decorrelated most.
4. **E4, substrate structure (mechanism 1).** With APL on and off, run `pn2kc_connections` ∈ {uniform, hemidraw, hemibrain wd20, hemibrain `prat_claws`, fafb-left, fafb-right} plus three shuffles of the hemibrain matrix passed through `_wPNKC`: Curveball degree-preserving (row and column sums kept, as in the thesis S4 null), KC-type-preserving (shuffle within α/β, α′/β′, γ), and glomerulus-frequency-preserving with real claw counts. Readout: Spearman between the model's deviations from the ORN prediction and the measured deviations (0.60 for wd20 today). Prediction if pair structure matters: real > type-preserving > degree-preserving > uniform. Vary `weight_divisor` (None, 10, 20, 40) and `dist_weight` to see whether synapse counts add anything; the repo note says `dist_weight` made things worse, which is itself informative.
5. **E5, KC heterogeneity (mechanism 1).** Compare uniform thresholds with `homeostatic_thrs=True`; `equalize_kc_type_sparsity` with `ab_prime_response_rate_target` above the others (α′/β′ are the most excitable type); a `fixed_thr` vector that scales with claw count (thr ∝ n\_claws^k, k ∈ {0, 0.5, 1}); `n_claws_active_to_spike` ∈ {2, 3} as a coincidence requirement; and per-type APL scaling through `_wAPLKC`/`_wKCAPL` (α′/β′ highest). Readout: Spearman with the control matrix and residual-vs-VCF. Prediction: heterogeneity increases deviations from the ORN prediction for the strongest odors without changing mean rate.
6. **E6, readout window (mechanism 4).** With `return_dynamics=True`, build matrices from spikes in \[0, 0.25\] s and \[0.25, 0.75\] s after odor onset and from a GCaMP-like kernel applied to spike trains (olfsysm currently counts spikes over the whole simulation including the 0.5 s pre-odor period, so a window option is needed in `run_KC_sims` or in Python from `spike_recordings`). Compare early vs late model matrices with the early vs late claw matrices of draft figure 7 and with the soma matrix; sweep `claw_tau` and `apl_taum`. Prediction: the late window should look more soma-like and more VCF-aligned than the early window only if feedback carries the remapping.
7. **E7, confound controls (item 7 above).** Push model outputs through the exact RDM pipeline (responsive-cell selection, chapter-3 logistic scaling of spike counts, Pearson on graded values), and build a density-matched null: shuffle KC identities within each odor keeping per-odor response counts, compute the expected correlation under independence, and report residual or partial correlations after regressing out per-odor density. Apply the same null to the real per-fly TNT and TNT3c data once available.
8. **E8, KC–KC inhibition (mechanism 5).** New term in `sim_KC_layer` (olfsysm.cpp ≈2389): subtract `wKCKC · activity(t−1)` at claws or somata with weights from `from_pratyush/2025-12-05/KCclaw-to-KCclaw_*`, scaled so that with APL off the response rate matches the TNT rate. Readout: does the APL-off model acquire the APL-independent residual structure (residual-vs-VCF 0.36)?
9. **E9, centrifugal structured input (mechanism 6).** Add an LHN layer from hemibrain PN→LHN weights (threshold-linear units) and LH-centrifugal and MB-C1 projections onto boutons and claws from the hemibrain, as an extra inhibitory drive in the bouton-model path. Readout: VCF alignment gained beyond APL, and whether it reproduces the APL-independent block effects (ep–6al, eb–t2h, paa–B-myr).
10. **E10, joint fit.** Extend `make_kc_data_loss` to take two target matrices (control, TNT) plus per-odor rates for both conditions, and fit threshold, APL scales, coupling and per-type scales with `optimize_mb_model_params`, with the TNT term evaluated at `wAPLKC_scale=0` and otherwise identical parameters. A fit that succeeds pins down the APL stage; a fit that fails tells you which pairs the substrate cannot produce, which is the list to take back to E4, E8 and E9.

## Model changes likely to move distances toward the data

The changes with the best expected payoff per unit of work are the ones that add constraints the model has never been fit to, before adding mechanisms. Ordered by that payoff:

**F****i****t to more than the mean response rate.** Today one scalar threshold and one APL scale are tuned to a mean rate; the rank structure is never fit. Use `make_kc_data_loss` with per-odor rates plus the correlation matrix, and add the TNT matrix as a second condition (E10). The lab's bouton and claw matrices are further stage-wise targets the model already produces internally (`bouton_sims`, `claw_sims`) but never compares.

1. **An APL stage shaped like the real one.** Per-claw connectome KC↔APL weights and PN-bouton↔APL weights are in the repo; APL units with coupling fit to data (E2) turn the single scalar into a spatial field. Two additions need C++: a saturating nonlinearity on each unit's potential (APL is non-spiking and switches from local to global activation with stimulus strength, Inada et al. 2017), and separate tuning of the KC→APL and APL→KC scales, which are currently moved together (`add_to_apl_weight_scales`). Constrain the KC→APL gain with the published scaling of APL response against PN input (Prisco et al. 2021) until the lab has its own APL imaging.
2. **Heterogeneous KCs from data in hand.** Per-KC thresholds that depend on claw count (hemibrain claws, Pratyush's segmentation) and per-type excitability and APL weights (α′/β′ most excitable and most APL-coupled, γ least; Inada et al. 2017), via `fixed_thr` vectors and `_wAPLKC`/`_wKCAPL`. Give the `unknown`-type KCs real weights rather than zero.
3. **A readout that matches the imaging.** Convolve model spike trains with a nuclear-GCaMP6s-like kernel with saturation (the chapter-3 logistic is a first version), take the peak over the lab's response window, and compute Pearson on that graded signal; count spikes only from odor onset. This removes a known mismatch before any mechanism is judged, and it is what E6 needs.
4. **Measured ORN time courses as input.** Replace the smoothed step with the lab's own ORN terminal traces (or a fitted adaptation model), so the late phase that feedback acts on is right; include all pebbled glomeruli rather than the connectome∩Task subset where ORN data exist.
5. **Glomerulus-specific inhibition in the AL.** The model's single global LN driven by mean ORN rate is a known simplification; the hemibrain LN→PN connectivity gives a glomerulus-weighted inhibition matrix, and the lab's PN-dendrite matrices (GH146) are the direct test of that stage. This matters less for the KC-specific remapping (PN dendrites barely move) but it sets how much drive contrast reaches APL (E3).
6. **Connections the connectome has and the model ignores.** KC-claw→KC-claw synapses (data already in `from_pratyush/2025-12-05`), MB-C1 and LH centrifugal inputs to the calyx (E8, E9). These are the only candidates on the list that could supply APL-independent, category-structured inhibition.

Data the lab is well placed to collect, each tied to a mechanism:

- **PN boutons and KC claws in APL>TNT flies on the same 16-odor panel** (mechanism 2 vs 3): tells whether APL's pair-specific effect is already visible at the claws (calyx action) or appears only at the soma (lobe action), and whether the bouton-level VCF shift is APL-dependent. An acute block (APL>shibire-ts or GtACR) alongside the 3-day TNT separates APL loss from homeostatic threshold changes.
- **Volumetric APL calyx imaging on the panel** (mechanism 2): odor-specific localization of APL activity parameterizes the units' drive and coupling directly instead of fitting them.
- **KC-subtype-resolved soma imaging** (α/β, α′/β′, γ drivers) on the panel (mechanisms 1 and 2): tests whether the remapping and the APL share are type-specific, as the food-PN convergence onto α/β and α′/β′ KCs predicts.
- **One indicator across compartments** (confound 7): soma with cytoplasmic jGCaMP8m or claws with a nuclear indicator in a few flies, to show the claw→soma change is not an indicator artefact.
- **Per-fly TNT and TNT3c matrices and per-odor response rates** (already collected): the noise floor for residuals of \~0.03 and the density-matched null (E7) both need them.
- **A concentration series for three or four panel odors in both genotypes** (mechanism 2): separates intensity-driven APL recruitment from identity-driven structure.

## Footnotes {#footnotes}

**How the calibrated readout works.** The model's side of every odor-odor distance is a cosine distance across KCs on raw spike counts (KCs x odors, the spikes in the response), and the data's side a cosine distance on z-scored nuclear GCaMP6s trial means, which compress large responses; on raw counts a few dozen high-firing KCs set the cosine (in the reference model, the 40 Hz fit at the control flies' 6.8 % response rate from E44, the top 1 % of KCs carry about two thirds of each odor's squared response vector). The readout used in the compressive readout of the model's spike counts (E65) and in the APL-effect fits (E67) replaces each spike count n by n / (n + k), with k the half-saturation count in spikes. Zero stays zero and the map is monotone, so which KCs respond (two or more spikes), every response rate and the realism bands are unchanged; only the weight of each responding KC in the cosine changes. At the reference model's k of 10.5 spikes, 2 spikes read 0.16, 5 read 0.32, 20 read 0.66 and 78 (its most active KC with APL on) read 0.88, so that KC weighs 5.5 times a 2-spike KC instead of 39 times. k is set per model and is not fitted to the APL effect: on the model's APL-on spike counts alone, it is the value (bisection on log k between 0.001 and 1000 spikes, 60 steps) at which the participation ratio of the readout equals the control flies'. The participation ratio of one odor's response vector over the N KCs is (Σx²)² / (N Σx⁴), the effective fraction of KCs that carry the odor's squared response (1 when all carry it equally, 1/N when one KC does); it is computed on responders only (model entries under 2 spikes and data trial means at or below 1 set to 0, for this statistic only), averaged over the 16 odors and, for the data, taken per recording and averaged over the 11 control-fly recordings. It is 3.4 % in the control flies (8.3 % with APL output blocked (TNT)) and 1.1 % for the reference model's raw APL-on counts; a smaller k evens the responders out and raises it, a larger k leaves the counts nearly raw. The same k is then applied unchanged to the model's acute APL-off counts (all entries, single spikes included), so the APL-off distances and the per-pair APL effect stay predictions, not fits. The calibration gives k = 10.5 spikes for the reference model and 4.5 for the first joint fit (E10), and 4.5 to 15 across the key models of E65; where the control flies' value cannot be bracketed the nearest bound is used (47 of the 728 archived variants). In the APL-effect fits (E67) k is calibrated again at every evaluation of the fit, on that evaluation's APL-on counts.

The participation ratio was chosen because it measures directly what the cosine weighs (how the squared response is spread over KCs) and is computed the same way on the model and on the data. The alternatives run in E65 agree with it: a power readout n^p calibrated the same way; calibrations to the control flies' responders-only top-1 % share of the squared response vector (48 %), to the coefficient of variation of the responders' amplitudes (0.48) or to the participation ratio on all values (6.1 %); and a rank-matching map of the model's APL-on counts onto the control flies' pooled responder amplitudes. For the reference model these give an APL-effect loss of 0.072 to 0.089, against 0.075 under the readout described here and 0.130 on raw counts (lower is better; the data's floor is about 0.04), and fixed, uncalibrated readouts of moderate compression (a square root; n / (n + k) at k of 10 or 20 spikes) give 0.075 to 0.076. The readout is a static, elementwise map: it has no indicator kinetics, no nuclear localization and no per-KC baseline or z-scoring (how the recordings were z-scored is an open question for Yang). Whether the main target is scored on raw counts or through this readout is Tom's decision; until then both are reported.
