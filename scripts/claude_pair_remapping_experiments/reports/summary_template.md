## What the data show: the ORN→KC remapping in control flies, and what blocking APL output does to it

Yang imaged Kenyon cell (KC) responses to 16 odors in seven chemical groups (fruity esters 2h, IaA, pa; small esters 2-but, eb, ep; aldehydes 6al, t2h; alcohols 1-8ol, 1-5ol, 1-6ol; ms; paa; terpenes -aPine, euc, B-myr) in control flies (11 recordings from 8 flies) and in flies whose APL output is blocked by tetanus toxin (8 recordings from 7 flies; "APL output blocked (TNT)" in the figures and tables).

For every pair of odors, the distance between the two odors' KC responses is one minus the cosine similarity of the two population response vectors (one entry per KC, trial-averaged), computed per recording and averaged over recordings; the 120 pair distances of a genotype form its 16 × 16 matrix. The same 16 odors exist in the lab's ORN data, so distances can also be computed between the glomerular response vectors at the input of the circuit ([paa, euc and -aPine are at substituted ORN concentrations](#fn-concentrations), a caveat that applies to every number below). For the ORNs every comparison in this page uses one minus the Pearson correlation across the 36 glomeruli imaged in both ORN panels (the glomeruli of the model's ORN input), computed per fly and averaged over flies where both odors were imaged in the same flies; the megamat and validation2 odors were imaged in different flies, so pairs across the two panels use fly-mean responses.

Cosine distance ranks the ORN pairs similarly (ρ=0.96 on odor-odor distances between the two) and gives a ρ on odor-odor distances with the KC matrices about 0.04 higher. The collapsible figure under the first one shows everything with Pearson correlation instead.

The statistics used throughout, in plain terms:

- **ρ on odor-odor distances:** the Spearman correlation between two sets of the 120 pair distances (a model matrix and a KC matrix, the two KC matrices, or a KC matrix and the ORNs): how far the order of the pairs agrees. A model's APL-on matrix is compared with the control flies' and its APL-off matrix with the APL output blocked (TNT) flies'.
- **Measurement ceiling:** the most any model can be expected to reach. Mean matrices of random halves of the recordings of one genotype agree at ρ={{data:ctrl_split_half_rho ~ .2f}} on odor-odor distances (control flies) and {{data:tnt_split_half_rho ~ .2f}} (APL output blocked (TNT)); corrected to the full set of recordings (Spearman–Brown), the square root of that reliability is the ρ a noise-free model would have with the observed mean matrix, {{data:ctrl_ceiling ~ .2f}} and {{data:tnt_ceiling ~ .2f}}. For the per-odor response rates the same construction gives {{data:ctrl_rate_rho_ceiling ~ .2f}} and {{data:tnt_rate_rho_ceiling ~ .2f}}.
- **Response rate ρ:** the Spearman correlation between the fraction of KCs responding to each of the 16 odors in a model and in the KC data (the per-odor response-rate pattern); the response rate of a model or a genotype is the mean of that fraction over odors.
- **Noise floor and the corrected distance:** two noisy response vectors are never parallel, so noise inflates every cosine distance toward 1. From the three trials of each odor, the distance between two independent trial means of the same odor is the noise floor ({{data:ctrl_trial_mean_floor ~ .2f}} in control flies, {{data:tnt_trial_mean_floor ~ .2f}} with APL output blocked (TNT); between single trials {{data:ctrl_single_trial_floor ~ .2f}} and {{data:tnt_single_trial_floor ~ .2f}}), and dividing each pair's cosine by the odors' signal norms (the trial mean's squared norm minus its noise share) gives the distance a noise-free recording would show. The order of pairs, the block and the isotonic R² are unchanged by the correction; only the level moves, and a deterministic model is compared with the corrected level.
- **Monotone relation and the block:** the control matrix [is fitted with the best monotone](#fn-isotonic) (isotonic) function of the APL output blocked (TNT) matrix; the fraction of variance it explains is the isotonic R², and a pair's residual from that curve says how far it deviates from the common trend. The block is the mean residual of the nine fruity × small-ester pairs.
- **Excess shared responders:** for each pair, the KC distance actually measured minus the distance expected if the two odors' responders were drawn independently with the odors' own response fractions; negative means the two odors share fewer KCs than their response density alone predicts, positive that they share more. Reported for the block pairs and for the other between-group pairs; the two numbers together are called the excess profile.
- **Structure beyond the ORN input:** the part of a KC matrix not explained by [a linear prediction from the ORN matrix](#fn-orn-residual) (the residual). Two references are used for it and are named separately below: the **structure vs wild-type somata** is the ρ of the residual with the same residual in [Remy's wild-type KC soma recordings](#fn-yang-remy) (the 12 odors shared with the megamat panel, 66 pairs); the **natural-source alignment** is the ρ of the residual with odor distances derived from natural-source odor statistics (the hyperbolic VCF embedding; the raw co-occurrence distances give no alignment, −0.09 / −0.10, so the result depends on the embedding).

**The remapping in control flies.** KCs push nearly every between-group pair to near-maximal distance: {{data:ctrl_n_gt08 ~ .0f}} of the 120 pairs are more than 0.8 apart in the control KC matrix, against {{data:orn_n_gt08 ~ .0f}} in the ORN input, while pairs within a chemical group stay partly similar (mean distance {{data:ctrl_within_mean ~ .2f}}). The order of pairs follows the ORN input only loosely (ρ={{data:rho_ctrl_orn ~ .2f}} on odor-odor distances, against a measurement ceiling of {{data:ctrl_ceiling ~ .2f}}): pairs that are close in the ORN input are spread over most of the KC range, and pairs already far apart in the ORN input are compressed at the top. This expansion is what any model of the circuit must produce first.

**What blocking APL output does, mostly: a uniform sparsening.** Twice as many KCs respond to each odor ({{data:tnt_frac ~ .3f}} instead of {{data:ctrl_frac ~ .3f}} of the KCs, with the per-odor pattern conserved), and the mean pair distance falls from {{data:ctrl_mean_dist ~ .2f}} to {{data:tnt_mean_dist ~ .2f}}, below the ORN input's own {{data:orn_mean_dist ~ .2f}}. The order of pairs is largely kept (ρ={{data:rho_ctrl_tnt ~ .2f}} on odor-odor distances between the two KC matrices) and the relation between them is monotone (isotonic R² {{data:iso_r2 ~ .2f}}, as high as the measurement ceiling allows).

**What it does not undo.** The code with APL output blocked (TNT) is more ORN-like than the control code (ρ on odor-odor distances with the ORN input {{data:rho_tnt_orn ~ .2f}} instead of {{data:rho_ctrl_orn ~ .2f}}), yet it keeps structure the ORN input does not predict: its natural-source alignment is {{data:tnt_vcf ~ .2f}}, which a linear model of the circuit does not reproduce. About half of the similarity gain with APL output blocked (TNT) is explained by the higher response density alone; the rest is structure, and both genotypes have fewer non-specific shared responders than a linear model gives (excess shared responders: control flies {{data:ctrl_excess_block ~ .2f}} for the block pairs and {{data:ctrl_excess_other ~ .2f}} for other between-group pairs; APL output blocked (TNT) {{data:tnt_excess_block ~ .2f}} and {{data:tnt_excess_other ~ .2f}}).

**The one pair-specific exception: the block.** The nine fruity × small-ester pairs, 0.51 apart in the ORN input and 0.88 in control flies, fall to 0.60 when APL output is blocked (TNT), well below the monotone trend. That residual, {{data:block ~ +.3f}} (90 % confidence interval {{data:block.lo ~ +.3f}} to {{data:block.hi ~ +.3f}}), is the block referred to throughout; it means APL removes a population of KCs that respond to both ester groups.

**What the data's trial-to-trial noise means for these targets.** Every model on this page is one deterministic run, while the KC matrices are means of three noisy trials. Putting trial-to-trial noise into the reference model at the level that reproduces the data's single-trial and three-trial floors, and passing ten pseudo-recordings through the data's own pipeline, shows three things. Fast noise on the PN rates cannot reach the data's floors at all (it averages out over the response window), while per-trial jitter of each KC's excitability or of each glomerulus's input gain can, at an unchanged response rate. Measured that way, the model's block shrinks by about a third, so a deterministic block equal to the measured one understates the data's noise-free block (the data's noise-corrected block is {{E43/onoff_noise.csv:model=data;matrix=corrected;transform=graded:block ~ +.3f}}). And noise lowers the model's mean distances rather than raising them, so the noise correction behind the corrected mean-distance targets above over-corrects model trials: those targets assume a noise the model's noise does not follow. The odor-odor order (ρ on odor-odor distances, isotonic R², response rate ρ) is unchanged by the noise.

**Re-fitting to the noise-corrected block does not help.** Re-fitting the reference model and the eight glomeruli at 1.25 with the loss's block target at the data's noise-corrected value moves their deterministic block by at most +0.01 and the block a recording of them would show by at most +0.004; pushing the target to +0.10 gets a deterministic +0.061 that still processes to +0.01. Every fit loses more than half of its block through the matched noise, and the loss is largest for the fit that is best deterministically (the eight at 1.25 at 70 Hz), so the processed block reverses the ordering the deterministic one gave: the model family's block is noise-fragile where the data's (itself a three-trial-mean measurement) is not. The best-models table below therefore carries the processed block beside the deterministic one, the recommendation to shrink the gain to 1.25 waits, and the open mechanism question is what would make a block carried by responses well above threshold rather than by marginal threshold crossings. One caveat on the re-fits: they were started from their counterparts by a seeding rule that reset the APL's bouton-input scale to the base model's value, so beside the target they also differ from their counterparts in the (unfitted) balance between the APL's bouton and KC inputs; the noise-robust block's fits start exactly at their seeds, and a clean repeat of these re-fits is pending.

## The model and its baseline

The model (olfsysm) runs ORNs → projection neurons (with antennal-lobe inhibition) → PN boutons (443 hemibrain boutons) → KC claws (hemibrain claws, about 9,500 claws on 1,732 KCs, about 6 claws per KC) → KC somata, with one APL neuron inhibiting claws and boutons through connectome synapse counts as weights, and somatic thresholds tuned so that 5 % of KCs respond to an odor. The bouton model with connectome APL weights is the baseline for everything below, and "the bouton model" means this model with whatever mechanism the row names added.

APL block is modelled as the acute knockout: the APL output weights set to zero with every other parameter unchanged. The flies express the toxin chronically, so a homeostatic re-tuning of the thresholds after the block is the other candidate comparison; the knockout baseline experiment ran both, and the data's doubling of the response fraction argues against full compensation, so the acute knockout is used throughout. One consequence is stated once here: in every tuned model the threshold is set so that twice the 5 % target respond with APL off, so an APL-off / APL-on response-rate ratio near 2 is built into the tuning, and only the fitted models' ratios are predictions.

## What made a difference

### The per-claw threshold on the PN→KC transfer: the decorrelation level and the excess sharing

The one KC-level manipulation with leverage was the claw-coincidence rule found in the KC heterogeneity experiment: a KC spikes when two or three of its claws are individually driven above a per-claw threshold. The per-claw saturation experiment then showed what the rule is. A pure cap on each claw's contribution (saturating or hard) moves every statistic the wrong way, whereas a rectified per-claw offset (each claw's input minus a threshold, floored at zero, summed linearly at the soma with the ordinary tuned somatic threshold) reproduces the coincidence models at the rule's own per-claw thresholds (ρ=0.92–0.95 on odor-odor distances between the two, on both wiring bases).

The rule is a threshold, not a bound: a KC should respond because a few of its claws are driven strongly, not because many are driven weakly, and the linear model's spurious shared responders are KCs of the second kind. The offset is a model parameter that keeps somatic integration, so it combines with the mechanisms below; "per-claw threshold" always means this offset, and "somatic threshold" the tuned threshold at the soma.

??? Biophysical realizations of the per-claw threshold
The threshold is about 40–70 Hz of PN rate above zero, roughly 1.5–2× a claw's spontaneous drive, and it must be present with APL output blocked (TNT). Candidates:

- a local nonlinearity in the claw itself: each claw is a discrete postsynaptic compartment wrapping one bouton, and a regenerative event there (Ca²⁺ / Na⁺ spikelet, NMDA-like voltage dependence) would make its output to the soma a threshold function of its input;
- subthreshold-activated K⁺ conductances in thin KC neurites (KCs express A-type Shal / Kv4 currents) that shunt small depolarizations, so only strong claw inputs propagate;
- presynaptic: PN→KC release that needs high-rate input (facilitating or priming-limited synapses), an input-rate threshold rather than a postsynaptic one;
- a tonic, subtractive inhibition of every claw from an APL-independent source (glia-derived GABA, or an APL output tetanus toxin does not block, such as electrical coupling), which with rectification is exactly an offset.

The counting rule's "2–3 claws" is then just the number of such events the somatic threshold needs.
???

**Does weak claw input have to count for nothing?** Letting a claw's input below the threshold count with a reduced slope, a quarter or a half, instead of nothing removes most of what the threshold does: the structure beyond the ORN input and the natural-source alignment fall steadily toward the model without a dead zone, the decorrelation level drops, and the weak odors are not recruited, since their PNs sit too far below the knee for a reduced slope to matter. What the slope buys is agreement with the APL output blocked (TNT) matrix, at the cost of more over-sharing among the esters with APL on, and at the fitted combined models it raises the extended loss ({{E41/stats_variants.csv:B_thr40_g0:xl_loss ~ .2f}} to {{E41/stats_variants.csv:B_thr40_g0.25:xl_loss ~ .2f}} with a quarter slope at 40 Hz). The per-claw threshold therefore works as a hard rectifier, which predicts that PN input below about 50 Hz contributes little to a claw's drive onto the soma, against the graded claw calcium reported in imaging.

### Breadth-weighted APL plus KC–KC inhibition: the block

The block needed a pair-specific removal, and the [oracle co-responder experiment](#fn-oracle-coresponders), the first of this page's two [oracle constructions](#fn-oracle), found what has to be removed: the KCs that respond to both ester groups when APL output is blocked (TNT) (8.5–11 % of KCs, broadly tuned, all with claws from the eight glomeruli both ester groups drive). Suppressing exactly those KCs gives the block, the control's excess profile, the best ρ on odor-odor distances with the control matrix of its base and a positive natural-source alignment, while suppressing random KCs or alcohol × aldehyde co-responders does nothing of the kind. The question became how a circuit finds them.

**Breadth-weighted APL** makes each KC's APL→KC weight proportional to a power of its tuning breadth (the number of odors it responds to with APL off, or, in the combined models and joint fits, its total spike count over the 16 odors with APL off): each claw's connectome APL→claw weight is multiplied by its KC's (breadth + 1) raised to the breadth exponent, and the overall KC↔APL scale is re-matched so that the number of responses with APL on is unchanged; the KC→APL weights are not reweighted. Every statistic moved toward the data and the non-specific excess sharing reached the data's level, but the block pairs kept sharing responders: breadth finds the broad KCs, and the remaining co-responders are not broad. A term for claws from the glomeruli shared by the two ester groups (the breadth × shared-input rule) got the block, but it is circular (only the block-specific glomerulus set works; scores defined from the whole panel fail) and anatomically backwards (hemibrain APL→claw synapse counts are *lower* on those glomeruli's claws).

**KC–KC inhibition** supplied the non-circular second ingredient: connectome-weighted inhibition between KCs whose claws contact each other, each KC's filtered spike output subtracted from its neighbours' somatic drive with a 50 ms time constant. Alone it is a weak second APL. On top of breadth-weighted APL it gives the block, the best ρ on odor-odor distances with the control matrix of any bouton model and the control's excess profile, with the per-odor response-rate pattern kept.

**Why it works.** With the threshold re-tuned, the term reassigns responses: the oracle's co-responder set supplies 55–67 % of the fruity / small-ester responses lost but gets only 25–42 % of those gained, so shared responders fall for the block pairs while other pairs keep theirs. A KC's lateral inhibition grows with how many of its connectome neighbours are co-active, which is largest in the dense, overlapping ester populations: the oracle's suppression without the oracle. The weight structure is part of it: with KC identities shuffled or uniform all-to-all weights, the generic effects (decorrelation, excess sharing, the APL-off matrix) are unchanged but the block falls to the noise floor, so the hemibrain's claw-to-claw contacts, which pair KCs sharing calyx neighbourhoods, are what make the term find the ester co-responders.

??? Biophysical realizations of breadth-weighted APL and KC–KC inhibition
*Breadth-weighted APL→KC synapses* (weight proportional to (1 + breadth)^k with k about 3): the natural implementation is activity-dependent synaptic scaling, KCs that fire to many odors having strengthened their APL→KC (and KC→APL) synapses by Hebbian or homeostatic plasticity over days; both synapse classes are plastic in the mushroom body. The intrinsic version (more active KCs raise their own threshold) identifies the same KCs but removes sharing proportionally everywhere, so it does not substitute; a purely structural version (broad KCs having more claws, hence more APL contacts) is too weak, since claw count does not matter in the wiring and heterogeneity experiments.

*KC–KC inhibition* with the connectome's claw–claw contacts, acting within about 50 ms:

- direct KC→KC synapses, which the hemibrain has in large numbers in the calyx and peduncle; KCs are cholinergic, but they express the inhibitory muscarinic receptor mAChR-A, which reduces KC odor responses, so cholinergic KC→KC transmission can be net inhibitory (the metabotropic timescale is slower than the model's 50 ms, which the model's onset-dominated response window may exaggerate);
- a KC→APL→KC loop confined to a calyx neighbourhood (APL's output is electrotonically local), admissible only if part of APL's local output survives tetanus toxin (gap junctions or non-vesicular release);
- ephaptic or extracellular K⁺ coupling among the tightly packed KC neurites and somata, a fast, activity-dependent and APL-independent lateral interaction;
- competition for a shared presynaptic resource at the bouton (dense co-active claws deplete it), which the bouton depletion experiment tested and ruled out as implemented.

The required property in all cases is co-activity dependence with the right partners: inhibition that scales with how many of a KC's calyx neighbours (the KCs whose claws contact its claws) are active, not with the KC's own breadth and not with the population as a whole; the first and third have that locality built in, the second only if APL's local output survives the toxin.
???

### APL inhibition of PN boutons: the odor-level drive dependence

Letting APL inhibit the PN boutons (connectome APL→bouton weights, about twice the fitted scale) gives the per-odor APL effect's dependence on ORN drive at matched response counts, with the pair-level statistics unchanged; more antennal-lobe contrast does not. Biophysically this is presynaptic GABAergic inhibition at PN terminals in the calyx (GABA-B receptors on PN boutons), which reduces release in proportion to the bouton's APL input; its effect is glomerulus-wise because a bouton's ~25 claws share it. The data-only confound controls later showed the odor-level effect is largely density, so this is a supporting rather than a primary ingredient; the bouton model is the APL-on reference for the mechanisms above, and the joint fit below shows the APL→bouton pathway is also what sets the APL-off / APL-on response-rate ratio.

### Glomerulus-specific claw strength: the weak odors, and the structure beyond the ORN input

The weak-odor analyses (below) had narrowed the five weak-input odors' problem to the claws of their own glomeruli counting for too little, and [the oracle test](#fn-oracle-gain) confirms it. With the per-claw threshold in place, scaling the PN → claw weights of the eight glomeruli that carry those odors (VA7m, DL1, D for ms; DM1 for B-myr and paa; DP1m, VC1 for euc and -aPine; DC2, DM5 for 1-8ol) by 1.5 recruits the weak odors with the fruity-ester overlap the fly's responders have, halves the over-removal of the block pairs' shared responders, gives the best ρ on odor-odor distances with the control matrix of any threshold model, and lifts the natural-source alignment to the data's level with APL output blocked and nearly to it with APL on, the first model to do so. The same gain on eight random glomeruli does none of this (it recruits nothing and disorders the code), so the effect is the identity of the glomeruli, not extra drive.

The costs are moderate: the strongly driven esters lose responders, so the per-odor response-rate pattern and the mean distance with APL on move away from the data, and the block stays small without breadth-weighted APL and KC–KC inhibition.

**What the gain is not: the anatomy.** Each claw weighted by its PN → KC synapse count, the anatomical version of claw strength the hemibrain offers, recruits paa and -aPine but not ms or B-myr, loses the threshold's natural-source alignment and doubles the over-sharing among the other pairs. The eight glomeruli are not special in anything the model has (their PN peaks rank 14th to 36th of 36, their claws per glomerulus and synapses per claw are ordinary), which is why the per-glomerulus peak normalization did not find them. A direct check of the model's calyx wiring against the hemibrain for these eight glomeruli closes the calyx anatomy as the substrate: PNs are missing from the wiring for most glomeruli (a median of 62 % present), but the [oracle set](#fn-oracle-glomeruli) is not worse off (63 % against 71 % for the others), and in the calyx output represented it is if anything over-represented (more boutons, 10.4 against 7.4 per glomerulus, and more claws per hemibrain calyx presynaptic site); the correction the anatomy would justify is 0.83 to 1.12, centred on 1, against the 1.5 the oracle gain needs.

**Nor the ORN → PN stage, applied uniformly.** Sweeping the model's antennal-lobe normalization and PN gain on the threshold base shows that weakening the normalization moves the weak odors away from the data (their top PNs fall, the esters' rise, and B-myr's responders go from 0.7 % of KCs to none), the default being already the strongest normalization the stage expresses. A uniform PN gain is the per-claw threshold in disguise: doubling every PN rate recruits the weak odors about as the 40 Hz threshold does (ms 2 %, B-myr 3 % of KCs with APL off) at the threshold's costs (the other pairs over-share, the mean distance falls to 0.73, the natural-source alignment is lost), and quadrupling it gives a dense code with no threshold left.

**What the oracle gain stands for** is therefore glomerulus-specific and upstream of the calyx: a glomerulus-specific ORN → PN gain, a release probability the synapse counts do not show, or, most simply, ORN input to these glomeruli that the lab's data understate for these odors by about 1.5× at the PN level, now with a concrete prediction of which glomeruli and by how much. The Hallem-input test (below) supports the last reading for ms and paa and not for B-myr.

**The gain in the combined model.** Put on the three-mechanism points, the gain does not add the way the threshold and KC–KC inhibition added to each other: at a fixed 5 % operating point it trades the block for the structure. **Without a per-claw threshold** the breadth-weighted APL plus KC–KC model reaches the data on every fitted target at once: the block at the data's size, the best ρ on odor-odor distances with the control matrix of any bouton model, the data's monotonicity, the APL-off / APL-on response-rate ratio and both per-odor response-rate patterns. It fixed the response-rate ratio by moving inhibition from the KC↔APL loop to the APL→bouton synapses: bouton-level inhibition is APL output too, so blocking APL then releases more responders, which confirms the hint from the bouton depletion experiment that the low ratio of the earlier KC–KC models was a matter of APL recruitment. The loss is nearly flat across breadth exponents 1 to 3, with different trade-offs (exponent 1 the best APL-off matrix and ratio, exponent 2 the best control matrix and per-odor response rates, exponent 3 the least KC–KC inhibition); without breadth weighting the fit still gets most of the block from KC–KC inhibition plus the APL re-balance but loses a little block and a tenth of the control-matrix ρ, which is what breadth weighting is worth in the fit.

**With a per-claw threshold** the joint fit is worse, even when started from the best no-threshold solution: the optimizer removes most of the KC–KC inhibition, both matrix ρ and the per-odor response rates fall, and the response-rate ratio is reached only with an extreme APL→bouton scale. What the threshold buys is the structure the loss does not contain: the structure vs wild-type somata, the natural-source alignment and the data's APL-on excess profile, where the no-threshold fits over-remove the block pairs' shared responders. Under these targets the threshold and KC–KC inhibition compete for the same KCs and the data prefer the latter. What no fit fixes: the APL output blocked (TNT) matrix's natural-source alignment and the block pairs' too-low sharing with APL off; the APL-blocked sharing diagnostic traces that to the model's input for five weakly represented odors, which no KC mechanism can repair.

### The joint fit with the structure targets

The same loss plus three structure terms (the structure vs wild-type somata, the natural-source alignment of the APL-off residual and the APL-on excess profile) selects a per-claw threshold, though which of the two is not resolved across wiring draws (see the replication below). Seeded from their earlier solutions at breadth exponent 2, the 40 Hz and 70 Hz fits reach extended losses of 2.45 and 2.38 against 2.83 without a threshold, entirely through the structure terms (1.09 and 0.94 against 1.74) and at a rising price in the fitted statistics (1.37 and 1.44 against 1.10).

### The combined model: the oracle gain inside the fit

With the gain of 1.5 on the weak odors' eight glomeruli held fixed and the four parameters re-fitted from the structure-target optima, the block and the gain coexist, which they did not at the fixed operating points: both threshold models keep the block (+0.049 and +0.043), reach the data's natural-source alignment ({{data:tnt_vcf ~ +.2f}}) and, for the first time in any fit, the data's order of the APL-on excess profile (the block pairs less over-removed than the other between-group pairs), with monotonicity back above the floor at 70 Hz and the best control-matrix ρ of any threshold model at 40 Hz (0.66). The extended loss falls to 1.94 and 1.93. The fit does it by raising the somatic threshold, shrinking KC–KC inhibition (to 11 and 22) and moving APL further onto the boutons.

The costs are the ones the fixed points showed and one more: response rate ρ with the control flies falls to 0.35 and 0.39 (the esters' response fractions drop as the weak odors are recruited), the structure vs wild-type somata falls from 0.54 to 0.32 and 0.36, and the mean APL-on distance falls to 0.74. The gain pays for what it buys almost entirely out of those two statistics.

**What the gain's response-rate cost is.** Comparing the per-odor response fractions of the fits with and without the gain against the KC data (figure below), the cost is confined to the APL-on condition: against the APL output blocked (TNT) data the gain costs nothing at 70 Hz and helps at 40 Hz. With APL on, three esters and three weak odors carry the loss. 2-but and 2h, the two odors the control flies respond to most, and IaA fall to ranks 10 to 14 of 16, because the gain fit's stronger APL removes 3.1 to 3.6 times as many responders as it leaves (the data: 1.8 to 2.4 times). ms, paa and B-myr, the odors the gain was meant to recruit, remain the three sparsest with APL on: the fit still removes 69 to 84 % of their APL-off responders (the data remove 52 to 59 %), although with APL off the gain brings them close to the data. And 1-8ol and euc, which the data do not place among the sparse odors, are over-recruited.

Behind this is a feature of the data that no fit reproduces: blocking APL output multiplies every odor's response fraction by about the same factor, 2.2, whatever the odor (the spread across odors is at the noise of the recordings), whereas every fit's APL is odor-selective. It removes the sparse odors' responders preferentially (without the gain, a factor 1.7 for the six esters against 2.8 for the weak-input odors and 12 for B-myr) and, with the gain, 2h, IaA and 2-but as well. The section on [what drives APL](#apl-drive) traces this to the model's APL input.

### Robustness: re-drawn wiring and the APL→bouton bound

**On re-drawn wiring.** Every joint fit above rests on one hemibrain wiring. Re-running the three structure-target fits on two re-draws of the bouton wiring (each KC type's claws re-assigned among that type's boutons, the smallest perturbation the wiring-substrate experiment used) keeps part of the conclusion and withdraws part. A per-claw threshold beats no threshold in five of the six comparisons across the three wirings, but which threshold wins flips (70 Hz on the real wiring and the first re-draw, 40 Hz on the second), so the loss selects a threshold and not a particular one. The threshold's gain in natural-source alignment repeats on every wiring; its +0.08 gain in structure vs wild-type somata does not (+0.01 to +0.05 on one re-draw, −0.03 on the other, inside the substrate experiment's ±0.15), and is withdrawn.

Every re-drawn fit is 1 to 2.5 worse in loss and 0.15 to 0.25 lower in structure vs wild-type somata than its real-wiring counterpart while keeping the block and the control-matrix ρ: fitted with the structure targets, the real hemibrain wiring carries structure the re-draws do not, so the absolute structure numbers of every fit on this page are the real wiring's. The same spread sets the scale for reading losses: differences of a few tenths between fits on one wiring are inside the variability between wirings.

### The reference model: the combined model with interpretable bounds

Re-fitting the combined model (the per-claw threshold, breadth-weighted APL, KC–KC inhibition and the 1.5× gain on the weak odors' eight glomeruli) with the APL→bouton scale capped at 3× the baseline costs nothing: both threshold fits improve on their uncapped versions (70 Hz 1.94 to 1.84, 40 Hz 1.93 to 1.73), with the bouton scale on the cap, the APL→KC scale back up, KC–KC inhibition smaller and the somatic threshold higher. Every gain of the uncapped fits is kept (the block at the data's size, the natural-source alignment at the data, the APL-on excess profile in the data's order, monotonicity 0.92 to 0.95) and the costs are smaller (response rate ρ with the control flies 0.41 / 0.47 against 0.35 / 0.39, the structure vs wild-type somata 0.35 / 0.40 against 0.32 / 0.36).

The capped 40 Hz model is therefore the reference model, with parameters that can be stated: somatic threshold 0.88× the baseline's (200 Hz), 40 Hz per-claw threshold, APL→KC 0.80×, APL→bouton 3.0×, KC–KC 18, breadth exponent 2, gain 1.5. Its loss is the lowest on this wiring, but by less than the spread between wiring draws, so it is the reference for its statistics (the block, the alignment, the excess profile, the control-matrix ρ), not for a rank among the capped fits. A gain of 1.25 does not split the trade but moves along it: it keeps the natural-source alignment and recovers most of the structure vs wild-type somata (0.49 / 0.50) while the block shrinks (+0.039 / +0.046) and the per-odor response-rate cost stays, so that cost belongs to the gain itself (a glomerulus-specific boost that lets the fit's APL remove the esters' responders, see above), not to its size.

### Which glomeruli the oracle gain needs {#gain-decomposition}

Splitting the reference model's gain by glomerulus, with every fitted scale held and the somatic threshold re-tuned to the fit's response rate, separates its two benefits. The natural-source alignment, and nearly all of what the gain does for the extended loss, come from one glomerulus, DC2: on its own it gives the alignment the full set gives (more with APL on), and without it the other seven give none and the structure against wild-type somata collapses. DC2 is not a weak-odor glomerulus. In the lab's ORN data it responds at {{lit:100–206 Hz # apl16_orn_deltas(), DC2 row: 2h, pa, 6al, t2h, 1-8ol, 1-5ol, 1-6ol}} to the alcohols, the aldehydes, 2h and pa, and weakly to the other weak-input odors, so what the structure rewards is a stronger input shared by the alcohols, the aldehydes and two of the fruity esters.

The weak odors are recruited by their own glomeruli (VA7m, DL1 and D for ms; DM1 for paa and B-myr; DP1m and VC1 for the terpenes, which they over-recruit), without the alignment, and each of those five is dispensable for the loss on its own; DM1 is not, since without it response rate ρ with APL on falls. The block needs DM5, which is also the glomerulus that lowers the structure against wild-type somata most. The low response rate ρ with APL on is not a cost of the gain: at the same fitted parameters without any gain it is the same. The 70 Hz fit shows the same pattern, except that DC2 alone loses the block there (extended loss {{E39/stats_variants.csv:thr70__only_DC2:xl_loss ~ .2f}} against {{E39/stats_variants.csv:thr70__full:xl_loss ~ .2f}} for the full set). The gain's structural part is therefore a single, strongly driven glomerulus, and the measurement question splits: the understated-input reading concerns the recruitment glomeruli, while for DC2 the question is whether its drive onto the KCs is stronger than its ORN response suggests.

### What drives APL: the per-odor suppression {#apl-drive}

Blocking APL output takes away the same share of every odor's responders in the data, and no model did that. Two things could make the model's APL odor-selective: the algebra of its inhibition, or what APL responds to. The divisive APL inhibition experiment tested both on the bouton model at 5 %.

**The algebra does not matter.** Replacing the subtraction with a division (the drive divided by one plus the inhibition) at the KCs, at the boutons or at both changes almost nothing. For a KC with a hard threshold that is expected: a drive divided by one plus the inhibition crosses threshold exactly when the drive minus the threshold times the inhibition does, so division acts as a shift of the threshold, as subtraction does.

**What APL responds to does.** In the bouton model, used by every model since the bouton base with claw coincidence, APL is driven by the summed input to all claws plus the bouton rates. Its activity then barely varies across odors: its peak during ms is three quarters of its peak during pa, though pa drives about a hundred times as many KCs. Every odor receives about the same threshold shift, and an odor whose few responders sit just above threshold loses most of them. Driving APL by the KCs' spikes instead makes APL follow how many KCs fire, and with the 70 Hz per-claw threshold the suppression becomes as uniform as the data's.

The statistic is the spread of the suppression: per odor, the ratio of the fraction of KCs responding with APL off to the fraction responding with APL on, and the standard deviation of its logarithm across the 16 odors (an odor with no responders counted as half a KC). The data give 0.15, where the noise of the recordings alone gives 0.13. Its ρ with the odor's APL-on fraction is negative when the sparse odors are suppressed most.

In every model here, APL off is the acute knockout described in the model section: the APL→KC and APL→bouton weights set to zero, nothing re-tuned. A model's APL-off value therefore depends only on its non-APL parts, and how its somatic threshold was chosen decides whether that value changes from one row to the next:

- **Threshold set with APL off.** The threshold is set so that twice the 5 % target respond with APL off, and the APL scale is then lowered until 5 % respond with APL on (the model's standard tuning). Models built this way on the same base share one APL-off code: the baseline, breadth-weighted APL and the doubled APL→bouton scale share the bouton model's, and the three forms of APL at 70 Hz share theirs. Their APL-off values are identical, and an APL mechanism's whole effect is in the APL-on column.
- **Threshold re-tuned with APL on.** Adding KC–KC inhibition or the glomerulus gain changes the drive, so the threshold is searched again until 5 % respond with APL on, with the APL scale held. KC–KC inhibition stays active with APL off, so these models' APL-off response rates and alignment differ from their base's.
- **Fitted jointly.** The threshold and the APL→KC, APL→bouton and KC–KC scales are fitted together against a loss that contains both conditions (5 % responding with APL on, an APL-off / APL-on response-rate ratio near the flies'). The threshold is then a compromise between the two conditions, and changing only the APL scales or their bounds (as in the APL→bouton bound control above) moves the APL-off values through the threshold and KC–KC strength the fit picks to compensate.

Read together, the two columns say what the APL-off score alone misses. The APL mechanisms change the alignment only through the APL-on column: breadth-weighted APL adds more than twice the flies' estimate, at the upper end of its interval, KC–KC inhibition raises both columns, doubling the APL→bouton scale does little, and APL driven by KC spikes or divisive at the KCs lowers it. Without the oracle gain the models get most of their alignment through APL, from an APL-off level well below the flies'. With the gain the APL-off value reaches the flies', and the model's APL then removes alignment instead of adding it: a little in the reference model, at the edge of the flies' interval, and clearly at the grid point with the gain. The flies' own APL contribution is uncertain, its interval reaching from about zero to the size of the models' contributions without the gain. No model reaches the flies' APL-on value.

## What did not matter, and what that rules out

- **Where APL acts spatially (local APL units):** 529 connectome-defined APL compartments at any coupling give the same geometry as one APL (ρ=0.997–0.999 on odor-odor distances between the two): the units carry odor-specific patterns, but each KC averages over about 6 claws spread through the calyx. Spatially local APL is not a mechanism at the KC level.
- **PN→KC wiring (the wiring substrate experiment):** no wiring (hemibrain claws, boutons, random, shuffled) reproduces the data's structure vs wild-type somata better than a 20-seed random null (0.27 ± 0.13); the real claw wiring scores below its own shuffles. The substrate is not the source.
- **How the model reads the hemibrain claw segmentation (multi-bouton claws and synapse-count claw weights):** 3.5 % of claws take synapses from two boutons of their PN; putting each in its dominant bouton instead of the lowest-numbered one, or splitting it, changes 3 % of the responding KCs and nothing else (ρ=0.997 on odor-odor distances with the default model). Weighting each claw by its number of PN→KC synapses (median 15, spread 0.43 of the mean, mostly within glomerulus) changes which KCs respond (79 % of responders) and raises ρ on odor-odor distances with the control matrix from 0.57 to 0.60 and with the APL output blocked (TNT) matrix from 0.69 to 0.73 (better; the ceilings are {{data:ctrl_ceiling ~ .2f}} / {{data:tnt_ceiling ~ .2f}}), a mild move toward fewer, stronger claws per KC, but leaves the block absent (−0.007; data {{data:block ~ +.3f}}) and adds no structure beyond the ORN input. The default construction is kept; the synapse weights are a base variant (used in the oracle glomerulus gain experiment's anatomical arm).
- **KC heterogeneity:** per-KC thresholds by claw count, per-type thresholds and per-type APL weights leave the geometry unchanged; homeostatic per-KC thresholds are degenerate at 5 % on 16 odors.
- **Readout window and time constants:** model spikes peak 50–150 ms after onset and die during the pulse; no window shows the block; APL and claw time constants only move the response rate.
- **Harder inhibition of the shared-drive claws, the bouton base with claw coincidence, panel-general glomerulus scores, a per-claw cap, bouton vesicle depletion:** each moved some statistics but not the block (depletion moved every statistic the wrong way), and the APL-synapse anatomy check (APL synapses anti-correlated with the glomeruli that need them) argues against any static, glomerulus-targeted APL rule.
- **Fixes for the weak-input odors** (per-glomerulus drive normalization, a softer per-claw knee, input scaling for the terpenes and 1-8ol, a supralinear per-claw transfer): each is negative; the weak-odor section below has what they changed and why.
- **Structured centrifugal input to the calyx:** the hemibrain's calyx-projecting LHCENT cells, driven through a lateral-horn layer of 1291 neurons built from the PN → LHN synapse counts, are broadly tuned (every cell to every odor, strongest for the small esters, at most a threefold range across odors), and MB-C1 takes 59 % of its input from KCs inside the calyx, so it is a calyx feedback neuron like APL (its output goes to the gamma KCs) rather than a lateral-horn relay. Inhibition at the KC somata at any strength changes nothing. Inhibition at the boutons is a glomerulus-specific gain cut on the VA4, DM1, DM4, DM3 and DP1m boutons that silences 2-but (0.113 → 0.014 of KCs with APL output blocked; the fly 0.193), B-myr (0.020 → 0.004; fly 0.105) and half of paa, and the pair effects and higher matrix ρ it produces (control 0.57 → 0.63; APL output blocked (TNT) 0.69 → 0.77) are what silencing those odors looks like: the per-odor response-rate pattern collapses (response rate ρ 0.68 / 0.80 → 0.31 / 0.45; ceiling {{data:ctrl_rate_rho_ceiling ~ .2f}} / {{data:tnt_rate_rho_ceiling ~ .2f}}), the APL-on block-pair excess goes from −0.42 to −0.58 (data −0.08), the block (−0.02) and the natural-source alignment (−0.04) do not appear, the small-ester / aldehyde divergences never move, and B-myr diverges from the other terpenes where the data converge. The pathway is not a source of category structure; MB-C1 would belong in a KC-driven, gamma-biased feedback model, a different question.
- **The algebra of APL's inhibition (divisive APL inhibition):** dividing the drive instead of subtracting from it, at the KCs, the boutons or both, leaves the per-odor suppression as selective as before; at a hard threshold division acts as a shift of the threshold (see [what drives APL](#apl-drive)).
- **The 5 % operating point:** fitting the reference model at the control flies' response fraction instead leaves its block, its agreement with both KC matrices and its structure where they were; it changes the response fractions, chiefly the weak odors' (see [the operating point](#operating-point)).
- **The data-only confound controls:** half of the APL-block similarity change is density; the isotonic R² of 0.94 is at the measurement ceiling (0.99 in a model is not a failure); the odor-level drive effect is confounded with density change; the only robust pair-specific signature is the block.

## Where the remaining gaps come from: five weak-input odors, and single-claw recruitment

Every model with the per-claw threshold shares too little among the non-ester pairs when APL output is blocked (TNT), and no fit repairs it. Three analyses located the problem, four model-side fixes failed, and the alternative input that exists was tried, which together narrow what is left to do.

**The diagnostic.** Comparing six archived models with the APL output blocked (TNT) data pair by pair, the missing sharing sits in the 70 pairs of five odors: ms, B-myr, euc, paa and 1-8ol. In the modelled ORN input these drive few of the 36 glomeruli, weakly (ms and B-myr, both at the exact concentration, reach 50 Hz in only three glomeruli; their largest ORN response is 73–79 Hz against a median of 150 Hz), so the models recruit 0.2–8 % of KCs for them with APL output blocked (TNT) where the fly recruits 10–14 %, and odors that recruit few KCs cannot share them. The ester, fruity-ester and aldehyde pairs are at or above the data's sharing. The per-claw threshold makes it worse: its response fractions with APL output blocked (TNT) follow the number of glomeruli an odor drives above 70 Hz (ρ=0.74, against 0.49 in the data), which is also the threshold's per-odor response-rate cost.

**What the fly does with them.** In Yang's recordings ms recruits a channel of its own: the narrowest and most exclusive responders of the panel (45 % of its control responders respond to nothing else, against 9–31 % for other odors), 11.6 % of KCs with APL output blocked (TNT), co-tuned with glomerulus D's ORN profile rather than with DL1 or with its own. In the hemibrain wiring 4.7 % of KCs carry two or more claws from DL1 or D and 7.6 % from DL1, VA7m or D, so at least a tenth of the fly's ms responders, and more if not every such KC responds, are recruited through a single claw of these glomeruli or through glomeruli the ORN data do not place among ms's. paa is a milder case of the same. The terpenes and 1-8ol are different: they are recruited through ordinary broad KC populations whose co-tuning follows their ORN profiles (right glomeruli, understated drive), except that B-myr's responders co-tune with the other terpenes in a way the ORN input does not predict.

**What this leaves.** The per-claw-transfer family is closed: a cap, a soft knee, a convex power and the hard knee itself all leave the weak odors unrecruited, and scaling their input recruits them without the fly's fruity-ester overlap. The reason is at the PN level (table below): the ORN-to-PN transform compresses rates so that no odor has an unusually strong PN; the weak odors drive two or three PNs above the knee where the esters drive five to seven, and any rule applied alike to every claw rewards breadth. What would recruit them is glomerulus-specific: claws from their glomeruli counting for more, which the oracle gain confirmed (above), while the hemibrain's per-claw synapse counts do not supply that gain and the centrifugal pathway does not either. The input side itself remains uncertain for the five odors (concentration, glomerular profile); the next subsection tests it with the one alternative dataset.

### Hallem's receptor data as the weak odors' input {#hallem-input}

The lab's ORN input and Hallem & Carlson's receptor data (odors at 10^-2; 16 of the model's 36 glomeruli) agree for the esters and aldehydes and disagree for exactly the weak-input odors: ms is a one-glomerulus odor in Hallem (DL1 at 258 Hz against 70 Hz in the lab's data), paa and 1-8ol peak in different glomeruli in the two datasets (DM6 against DM1; VC3 against DC2), B-myr is weak in both, and euc is not in Hallem's set. Putting Hallem's values for ms, paa, B-myr and 1-8ol into the input (on the shared glomeruli, the lab's elsewhere) on the 70 Hz threshold model, one odor at a time, says which of the five odors' deficits the input can explain. Hallem's 10^-2 is ten times Yang's concentration for ms, paa and 1-8ol, so these inputs are upper bounds.

**By odor.** Hallem's ms recruits half the fly's ms population with APL off and Hallem's paa recruits paa at the fly's fraction, both at no cost to any other statistic; Hallem's 1-8ol over-recruits and costs the natural-source alignment; Hallem's B-myr is weaker than the lab's and changes nothing. Every ms responder recruited by DL1 has a second claw from a glomerulus ms drives weakly: one claw at 258 Hz does not fire a KC in this model, so the recruited population is narrower than the fly's with APL off (a breadth of 2.4 odors against 4.9), and the fly's single-claw ms channel, if it is one, needs a mechanism the model lacks.

**In the reference model**, which carries the 1.5× gain on DL1, VA7m and D, Hallem's ms brings ms to the fly's fraction with APL off, but APL then spares the new narrow responders (blocking APL output multiplies ms's response fraction by 1.3, against 2.1 in the fly), because breadth-weighted APL inhibits broad KCs most: a narrow channel is where that rule and the fly part.

**Level is not profile.** Scaling the lab's profiles of the four odors to Hallem's level instead over-recruits ms, paa and 1-8ol (16 glomeruli above the knee for ms instead of two) and gives the highest ρ on odor-odor distances with the APL output blocked (TNT) matrix of any model so far with the per-odor response-rate pattern destroyed: the matrix ρ rewards a weak odor's recruitment whatever its size, which is why it is read together with response rate ρ.

### The operating point: the control flies' 6.8 % instead of 5 % {#operating-point}

Every model on this page is tuned or fitted so that 5 % of KCs respond with APL on, a convention from the lab's earlier modelling, while {{data:ctrl_frac ~ .1%}} of the control flies' KCs respond (and {{data:tnt_frac ~ .1%}} with APL output blocked). Re-fitted with the response-rate target at the control flies' fraction instead, the reference model keeps everything the fit measures: the block, ρ on odor-odor distances with both KC matrices and the structure vs wild-type somata stay where they were at 5 %, response rate ρ with APL on rises slightly, and the loss without its response-rate term is no worse (the fit does it with a stronger APL↔KC loop and a weaker APL→bouton scale). Moving only the somatic threshold to the new operating point, with every other fitted scale held, costs the 40 Hz model almost half of its block, so a threshold-only change of operating point is not neutral. What the operating point does change is the response fractions: at 6.8 % the weak-input odors respond more, and the reference model, whose gain was fitted at 5 %, now over-recruits them with APL off. Its weak odors were already too strong relative to the mean at 5 % (the absolute match there came from an overall APL-off response rate below the data's), so the gain of 1.5 on eight glomeruli is more than the data's operating point needs. In the bouton bases tuned from 3.4 % to 10 %, ρ on odor-odor distances barely moves and the block stays at zero; the weak odors' share of the mean APL-off response rate rises from {{E44/comparison.csv:A_thr70_t0.050:weak_share_off ~ .2f}} at 5 % to {{E44/comparison.csv:A_thr70_t0.068:weak_share_off ~ .2f}} at 6.8 % in the 70 Hz base (data {{data:tnt_weak_share ~ .2f}}), a fifth to a quarter of the way, and the APL-off mean pair distance falls toward the data's as more KCs respond ({{E44/comparison.csv:A_thr70_t0.050:mean_dist_off ~ .2f}} to {{E44/comparison.csv:A_thr70_t0.068:mean_dist_off ~ .2f}}; data {{data:tnt_mean_dist ~ .2f}}). Fitting at the data's fraction costs nothing and compares response fractions with the data at the same mean; the gain is the parameter to re-fit there.

**The gain at the data's operating point.** Re-fitting the gain arms at the control flies' response rate settles three things. The gain is not an artefact of the 5 % convention: without it the fits keep the block but lose the APL-off to APL-on response-rate ratio and the natural-source alignment with APL off, at a much higher loss. At the 70 Hz per-claw threshold a smaller gain on the eight glomeruli (1.25) gives the best fit of all, keeping the block and the agreement with both KC matrices with more structure against wild-type somata; at 40 Hz the block still needs 1.5. And the three-glomerulus core with Hallem's ms input is the best fit at 40 Hz and the only arm whose weak odors take the data's share of the APL-off response, at its usual cost in agreement with the KC matrices. The differences are within the spread between wiring draws, so the reference model is unchanged until a second draw.

**The operating-point fits on a re-drawn wiring.** The two best fits at the control flies' response rate, the eight glomeruli at 1.25 at the 70 Hz per-claw threshold and the DC2, DM5 and DM1 core with Hallem's ms input at 1.5 at 40 Hz, led the reference model by only 0.1–0.2 of extended loss, within what a re-drawn wiring can move a fit. Re-fitted on the second within-type claw-permutation draw (the one the replication on re-drawn wiring and the reduced gain sets on a re-drawn wiring used), both orderings hold and widen: at 70 Hz the eight at 1.25 reach {{E46/comparison.csv:thr70_wiring-claw_permutation_within_type-seed2_gain1.25_rate0.068_k2_cap3:loss ~ .2f}} against {{E46/comparison.csv:thr70_wiring-claw_permutation_within_type-seed2_gain1.5_rate0.068_k2_cap3:loss ~ .2f}} for the eight at 1.5, and at 40 Hz the core reaches {{E46/comparison.csv:thr40_wiring-claw_permutation_within_type-seed2_input-hallem_shared_ms_gain1.5_rate0.068_k2_core3_cap3:loss ~ .2f}} against {{E46/comparison.csv:thr40_wiring-claw_permutation_within_type-seed2_gain1.5_rate0.068_k2_cap3:loss ~ .2f}}. The draw again takes most of its toll on the block: the reference model keeps half of its block on the draw at 70 Hz and two thirds at 40 Hz (the latter from a second start with a lower somatic threshold and a stronger APL; from its own start it kept a third), the core two thirds, and the eight at 1.25 nearly all of it, by shifting to that lower-threshold, stronger-APL solution. Structure against wild-type somata and the natural-source alignment fall on the draw for every model, as in the replication on re-drawn wiring, while agreement with the two KC matrices does not. So the smaller gain at 70 Hz and the core at 40 Hz are not artefacts of the real wiring, and fitting at the data's response fraction left the reference model's dependence on the real wiring unchanged; what that dependence consists of is taken up [next](#wiring-feature).

### Which feature of the wiring the fitted block needs {#wiring-feature}

Every re-drawn wiring on this page is one kind of shuffle, each KC type's claws re-assigned among that type's boutons, and every such re-draw lost part of the block once the model was fitted with the structure targets. Which feature of the real wiring the block needs was asked with four kinds of shuffle of the bouton wiring: claw permutations, which keep each KC's and each glomerulus' number of claws, and Curveball shuffles, which keep each KC's number of distinct glomeruli and each glomerulus' number of KCs, each either across all KCs or within each KC type (so that each type keeps its glomerulus repertoire). The reference model at the control flies' response fraction (the eight glomeruli at 1.5, at both per-claw thresholds, and the eight at 1.25 at 70 Hz) was first run on five draws of each kind at its fitted APL and KC–KC scales, with only the somatic threshold re-tuned to the response fraction (it moved by at most 2 %), and then re-fitted on one draw of each kind.

At the fitted scales every kind of shuffle loses part of the block, and the draw-to-draw spread is as large as the differences between kinds, so no kind separates from the others: the within-type Curveball keeps the most for the eight at 1.5 and the claw permutations the least at 40 Hz, but the eight at 1.25 reverse that order. Agreement with the two KC matrices does not move; what the shuffles cost besides the block is the APL-on response-rate pattern and the natural-source alignment. Re-fitted, the block comes back, but by an amount set by the draw and not by the kind of shuffle. On the first draw the two shuffles that ignore KC type made the data's block in full, on a wiring whose every claw was re-assigned at random across all KCs, and the two within-type shuffles kept the least; the follow-up on four draws each of the plain claw permutation and the within-type Curveball (the fitted block on more shuffle draws) found the two kinds indistinguishable, their means in the opposite order and both between half and three quarters of the data's block, with single draws recovering anything from none of it to all of it, none above the real wiring's value, and the four draws with the lowest loss carrying the four largest blocks. So the fitted block does not need the hemibrain's pairing of glomeruli on KCs: a re-fit recovers it on a random wiring with these claw counts to a degree that varies from draw to draw, and the re-draws' loss of it above was the typical draw's loss (their within-type claw-permutation draws recovered what a typical draw of either kind recovers), not a sign that the block rides on the real wiring. What every shuffle loses, re-fitted or not, is the natural-source alignment with APL off, which on the real wiring sits at the data's value and on no shuffled wiring the model was re-fitted on comes close to it; APL's effect on it (on minus off) is at or below zero on the real wiring and on the within-type shuffles, where the data's is positive, and positive only on the plain claw permutation, the one statistic that told the two kinds apart. The structure against wild-type somata is kept by every shuffle for the eight at 1.5 and lost only for the eight at 1.25. The real wiring's own contribution to the fitted models is therefore the natural-source alignment, not the block; a block difference between two wirings means something only once several draws of each have been re-fitted.

### The per-claw threshold at low concentration {#low-concentration}

Every constraint above comes from the APL16 panel at 10^-3 to 10^-2, where the per-claw threshold that every good model carries costs nothing. Two further KC datasets of Yang's have weak input by construction: in control flies 2h and ma at 10^-7 recruit 5–6 % of KCs and farn at 10^-3 4 %, and with APL output blocked two to three times as many. Run at their fitted parameters on those odors (ORN input within one log unit of the recorded concentration, the stronger one where the exact one is missing, so a failure to recruit is the conservative result), the fitted models recruit a half to a tenth of the KCs the flies recruit and none for ma, whose ORN input is exact; the matched trial noise roughly doubles the fractions without closing the gap, and with APL off the models are as far short of the flies with APL output blocked, so the deficit is in what reaches the KCs through the per-claw threshold, not in APL (whose share, the APL-off to APL-on ratio, is about the flies' for 2h and farn). The model with the lowest somatic threshold, the eight glomeruli at 1.25 at 70 Hz, comes closest. The flies' binary mixtures recruit fewer KCs than the union of their components, mildly in control flies and strongly with APL output blocked (ma + farn fewer than either component); with the components' ORN input combined as their maximum per glomerulus the models show the mild sub-additivity and not the strong one. So the per-claw threshold, a hard rectifier at 40–70 Hz of PN input, is falsified at low concentration: a sub-threshold slope or noise through the threshold has to carry these responses, and the fitted models describe KC recruitment at 10^-3 to 10^-2 only.

## Experiments running or not yet run, and what each would tell us

- **The open measurement question** is the ORN responses of the oracle glomeruli to ms, paa, B-myr, euc and 1-8ol at Yang's concentrations; after the Hallem-input test it is two glomeruli first, DL1's response to ms and DM6's to paa at 10^-3, which would decide whether those two odors' deficit is in the input, while B-myr's and euc's stay open. The decomposition of the gain adds that its structural part needs a measurement of a different kind: DC2's drive onto the KCs relative to its ORN response ([above](#gain-decomposition)).
- **Model and readout changes still worth making:** a saturating APL unit nonlinearity, a GCaMP-like readout with saturation, measured ORN time courses, real weights for the KCs of unknown type, and, from the weak-odor analyses, better-constrained ORN input for ms, B-myr, euc, paa and 1-8ol (concentrations and glomerular profiles; Hallem's receptor data, [tried above](#hallem-input), cover ms and paa but not B-myr or euc, and at ten times Yang's concentration).
- **Data that would discriminate the mechanisms:** APL imaging under the same odors (is APL's output to broadly tuned KCs larger, as breadth-weighted APL requires?), KC–KC interaction tests (KC responses with neighbouring KCs silenced, the direct test of KC–KC inhibition), per-trial KC data for a reliability readout (and for the noise floor of the mean distances), and, for the single-claw question, imaging of ms responders with their glomerular input identified.


- **The noise-robust block (running).** The block target through the noise showed that the fits' block is made by KCs near threshold and shrinks to a quarter or a half once the model is run with the data-matched trial noise, whatever the target. This asks whether the mechanisms in hand can make a block that survives that noise: first where the fitted block lives (which KCs carry each pair's excess, and their threshold margins; which pairs are stable across pseudo-recordings), then joint fits whose block term is computed on jittered runs (fixed draws, common random numbers), re-scored through the pipeline. If the noise-fitted models reach a processed block near the data's at little cost, the fragility was the fitting criterion and the fits move to the noise-aware loss; if the processed block stays below half the data's, the mechanisms in hand cannot make a noise-robust block of the data's size, and the pairs that stay negative name the next mechanism to try.

## Parameter glossary with mechanisms

- `claw_thr` (per-claw rectified offset; replaces the coincidence count `n_claws_active_to_spike`): claw-level nonlinearity, subthreshold K⁺ shunting, release-rate threshold, or tonic APL-independent inhibition; see the per-claw threshold section.
- Breadth-weighted `_wAPLKC` (exponent k): activity-dependent scaling of APL↔KC synapses; see the block section.
- `kc_kc_inh`, `kc_kc_tau` (KC–KC inhibition, connectome claw–claw weights): direct KC→KC transmission via inhibitory muscarinic receptors, toxin-insensitive local APL loops, ephaptic / K⁺ coupling, or shared presynaptic depletion; see the block section.
- APL→bouton weights (`wAPLPN`, `wPNAPL`): presynaptic GABA-B inhibition of PN terminals; see the bouton section; the joint fit raises them 2–3× to get the response-rate ratio.
- `fixed_thr` (somatic threshold, tuned to 5 %): KC intrinsic excitability under homeostatic control; the tuning procedure is a homeostatic sparseness set-point, and "APL off = 2× response rate" is what that set-point implies.
- `wAPLKC`, `wKCAPL` scales: global gains of the APL↔KC synapses (the data want APL to roughly halve the responder count).
- `n_spikes_for_response = 2`: the imaging readout (nuclear GCaMP6s needs more than one spike); the readout-window experiment found the window itself is not a knob.
- `claw_tau`, `apl_taum` (50 ms): synaptic / membrane filtering at claws and in APL; only the response rate depends on them.
- Glomerulus-specific PN → claw gain (an oracle: the wiring columns of VA7m, DL1, D, DM1, DP1m, VC1, DC2, DM5 scaled by 1.5): the claws of the weak odors' glomeruli counting for more; realizations in the glomerulus-specific claw strength section (bouton size or release sites per glomerulus, ORN → PN gain, claw-level scaling, or understated ORN input).
- `claw_pow`, `claw_pow_ref` (convex per-claw transfer after the threshold): a regenerative claw (dendritic spike, NMDA-like supralinearity); tested and negative, because it rewards the esters' breadth rather than the weak odors' few claws; see the weak-input section.
- `ext_inh_kc`, `ext_inh_bouton`, `ext_inh_scale` (odor-specific external inhibition of KC somata and of bouton output during the odor): the lateral-horn centrifugal and MB-C1 pathway built from hemibrain synapse counts; tested and negative (too flat across odors at the soma; a glomerulus-specific gain cut at the boutons).
- `claw_sat` (per-claw cap), `bouton_ves_p` / `bouton_tau_r` (bouton depletion): compressive transfers; both go the wrong way. Biophysically real (receptor saturation, short-term depression at PN terminals), but they cannot be what makes the KC code sparse in the data's way; whatever they do in the fly must sit under a per-claw threshold.
- `apl_unit_coupling`, APL units: spatially local APL; no effect at the KC level.
- `pn_inhadd` / `pn_inhsc`: antennal-lobe lateral inhibition (PN contrast); no help.

## Footnotes {#footnotes}

### How the monotone (isotonic) fit is done, and why {#fn-isotonic}

**What is fitted.** The 120 pair distances of the control matrix are regressed on the 120 pair distances of the APL output blocked (TNT) matrix with isotonic regression: the fitted curve is the non-decreasing step function that is closest to the control values in the least-squares sense (the pool-adjacent-violators algorithm; scikit-learn's IsotonicRegression with increasing=True). "Isotonic" means order-preserving: the curve may bend any way as long as it never goes down, so a pair that is farther apart with APL output blocked is predicted to be at least as far apart in control flies. The isotonic R² is the fraction of the control variance the curve explains (0.94); a pair's residual is its control distance minus the curve's value at its APL output blocked (TNT) distance; and the block is the mean residual of the nine fruity × small-ester pairs ({{data:block ~ +.3f}}), with its confidence interval from resampling recordings.

**Why this fit.** The hypothesis being tested is that APL's output is a uniform sparsening: every pair moves in the same direction by an amount that depends only on how far apart the pair already is. Any such effect is a monotone map from the APL output blocked (TNT) matrix to the control matrix, and isotonic regression is the most general monotone map, so its residuals contain only what no uniform effect can produce, the pair-specific part. It assumes nothing about the shape of the map (the data's map is strongly compressive at the top, where KC distances saturate near 1), it has no tuning parameter, and its R² can be compared directly with the measurement ceiling computed the same way between halves of the recordings.

**The alternatives, and why not.** A linear fit of control on APL output blocked (TNT) assumes the change is proportional; with the saturation near 1 it explains less of the control variance (R² 0.85 against 0.94) and leaves curved residuals, so its block estimate (+0.098) mixes the pair-specific effect with the curvature the line cannot follow. A parametric saturating curve (a power or logistic function) has fewer degrees of freedom but imposes a shape the data would have to meet, and the block estimate then depends on that choice. Rank correlation alone (Spearman 0.87) tests monotonicity but gives no per-pair residual, so it cannot locate the block. Comparing the two matrices with the expectation from response density (the confound controls) asks a different, complementary question (whether two odors share more KCs than their response fractions predict) and is reported separately as the excess shared responders. The isotonic fit's own limitation is that a step function through 120 points can follow local noise, so its R² is an upper bound for any smooth monotone curve; that is why the residual is read for a block of nine pairs rather than for single pairs, and why the measurement ceiling is computed with the same fit.

### How "structure beyond the ORN input" is computed {#fn-orn-residual}

1. **Subset odor distances to megamat.** Since Yang's APL16 panel only includes 12 of the 17 odors in Remy's megamat panel (its other 4 odors come from validation2), we subset the ORN / KC / embedded-VCF odor-odor distances down to these 12 odors, leaving us {{data:n_ref_pairs ~ .0f}} pairs of the 120 in Yang's full APL16 panel.
2. **Linear prediction.** For KC odor-odor distances, from one model or the mean over per-recording distances, fit KC distance = a + b × mean ORN distance across the {{data:n_ref_pairs ~ .0f}} pairs by ordinary least squares. Each set of KC distances gets its own a and b. For the control flies, b = {{data:ctrl_orn_fit_slope ~ .2f}}, a = {{data:ctrl_orn_fit_intercept ~ .2f}}, and R² = {{data:ctrl_orn_fit_r2 ~ .2f}}.
3. **Residual.** Each pair's KC distance minus the line's prediction. Because a and b are fitted to each set of KC distances, any part of those distances that is a straight-line function of the mean ORN distances goes into the line. That includes a uniform offset, such as a higher overall level of decorrelation, and a uniform stretch. The residual keeps only how individual pairs depart from that line, and by construction it is uncorrelated with the mean ORN distances.
4. **Comparison.** ρ between the residual and either the residual of Remy's wild-type KC soma matrix, computed the same way (structure vs wild-type somata: {{data:ctrl_struct_wt ~ .2f}} for the control flies), or the hyperbolic natural-source distances themselves, including the {{data:n_vcf_filled ~ .0f}} pairs whose raw natural-source value is missing and is filled by the embedding (natural-source alignment: {{data:tnt_vcf ~ .2f}} with APL output blocked (TNT)).

A dependence on the ORN distances that is curved but monotone, as a threshold produces, also leaves a systematic residual, which this statistic counts as structure beyond the ORN input: in the left panel, the within-group pairs with small ORN distances all sit below the line.

### What "oracle" means on this page {#fn-oracle}

An oracle, here, is a model manipulation that is handed part of the answer. Instead of a circuit rule the fly could implement, the manipulation is given a list of exactly which KCs or which glomeruli to change, read off the KC data or off the model's own output scored against the data, and changes exactly those. Its result is a ceiling: an existence proof that changing those elements would be sufficient, made meaningful by controls that make the same change to random KCs or random glomeruli and show that the identity of the elements is what matters. It is never a mechanism; the question each oracle leaves behind is what in the circuit could single out those elements. The page uses two oracles, the oracle co-responder suppression and the oracle glomerulus gain, each with a footnote below, and one partial one: the breadth × shared-input APL rule's shared-claw term used glomeruli chosen from the two ester groups themselves, which the panel-general rule was then built to avoid. The progress plot leaves all of these out (the co-responder suppression, the gain's glomerulus subsets in the decomposition, the block-specific glomerulus set), and with them every model that carries the glomerulus gain at all, whether applied at fixed parameters or held fixed inside a fit (the combined-model fits, the reference model and the operating-point fits included), because the best of them on any statistic would be a selection made with the data in hand: on that plot the best so far is always a model given nothing from the KC data but its fitted parameters. The best-models table, by contrast, shows the gain-carrying fits beside the rest, with the gain named in their headers.

### The oracle co-responder suppression {#fn-oracle-coresponders}

**What was done.** On the claw-dynamics base, with and without the coincidence rule, the model was tuned with APL on and run with APL off, and the co-responders were defined from that APL-off run as the KCs responding to at least one fruity ester (2h, IaA, pa) and to at least one small ester (2-but, eb, ep). The connectome APL→KC weights of those KCs' claws were then multiplied by a factor (4 in the variant this page quotes; 8, and 1000, which leaves APL acting on those KCs alone, were also run), the APL re-tuned to the 5 % response rate, and the model scored with APL on and off like every other variant.

**Why it is an oracle.** The set of KCs to inhibit was chosen by the outcome wanted. The block is the pair-specific separation of the fruity esters from the small esters, and these are exactly the KCs that keep the two groups' representations close; no circuit rule picked them, the model's own output did, read against what the data show.

**The controls.** The same factor on an equal number of random KCs outside the set, and on the co-responders of a different pair of odor groups (the alcohols and the aldehydes); neither produces the block.

**What it established.** Removing exactly those KCs is sufficient for the block at the measured size, with the control flies' excess profile and the best ρ on odor-odor distances of its base, so the mechanisms that followed (the breadth × shared-input rule, KC–KC lateral inhibition) were judged by how much of this suppression they reproduce without being told the set. "The oracle's suppression without the oracle" is the KC–KC term achieving most of the same removal through the connectome's claw-to-claw contacts.

### The oracle glomerulus gain {#fn-oracle-gain}

**What was done.** In the PN→KC wiring, the columns of eight glomeruli (VA7m, DL1 and D for ms; DM1 for B-myr and paa; DP1m and VC1 for euc and -aPine; DC2 and DM5 for 1-8ol) are multiplied by a factor, 1.5 on this page unless a column says 1.25 (2 and 3 were also run in the original experiment), so every claw a KC receives from those glomeruli counts that much more in its summed input: a PN→claw gain, applied before the per-claw threshold. The somatic threshold is then re-tuned to the target response rate and, in the fitted models, the APL scales and the KC–KC scale are fitted with the gain held fixed.

**Why it is an oracle.** The glomeruli were chosen as the ones the five weak-input odors drive most in the lab's ORN data, after those five odors had been identified as the ones the threshold model fails to recruit relative to the KC data. The set therefore encodes which odors the model was known to miss, and the factor 1.5 was set by hand to recruit them, not derived from anything in the model or the anatomy.

**The controls.** The same gain on eight random ORN-driven glomeruli outside the set (it recruits nothing and disorders the code), and the anatomical alternative, each claw weighted by its PN→KC synapse count (it recruits two of the five odors and loses the alignment).

**What it stands for.** Since the identity of the glomeruli carries the effect, not the extra drive, and the calyx anatomy of those glomeruli is ordinary (the substrate check), the gain is read as a proxy for something glomerulus-specific upstream of the calyx: an ORN→PN gain, a release probability the synapse counts do not show, or ORN input the lab's data understate for these odors by about that factor at the PN level; the Hallem-input test supports the last reading for ms and paa.

**Where it lives on.** The gain is a fixed setting of every combined model, of the reference model and of the operating-point fits, and the decomposition and the reduced-set fits ask which of the eight glomeruli it needs (next footnote).

### The oracle glomeruli as a set {#fn-oracle-glomeruli}

The eight glomeruli of the oracle gain (VA7m, DL1, D, DM1, DP1m, VC1, DC2, DM5) are also treated as a set in three later analyses. The substrate check compared their representation in the model's calyx wiring (PNs present, boutons, claws per presynaptic site) with the other ORN-driven glomeruli's, to ask whether the hemibrain's calyx anatomy could justify a gain of 1.5 for them; it cannot, the correction the anatomy would justify being centred on 1. The decomposition split the gain by glomerulus at the reference model's fitted parameters and found that its structural part, the natural-source alignment, comes from DC2, which is not a weak-odor glomerulus, while the five weak odors are recruited by their own glomeruli and the block needs DM5. The reduced-set fits then put DC2 alone, or DC2 with DM5 and DM1, into the joint fit in place of the full set. In each of these the set is still the oracle's: asking which members it needs does not make the choice of the set less of an oracle, which is why the decomposition's subsets are left out of the progress plot (the best subset per statistic would be a selection made with the data in hand). The open measurement question names the same glomeruli: the ORN responses of the oracle glomeruli to the five weak-input odors at the KC data's concentrations.
