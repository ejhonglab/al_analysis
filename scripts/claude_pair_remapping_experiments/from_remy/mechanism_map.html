Claw Dynamics Mechanism Map

What the three components of “KC claws beyond E[KC claws | PN boutons]” stand for, and where in the circuit each could come from

Top: the three parts of the claw signal that the analysis separates, each with the pattern it makes in the odor-pair matrix and its signature in the data. Bottom: one calyx microglomerulus, drawn with the seven places a mechanism can act. Coloured dots on each place say which component that mechanism would feed.

Odor partA per-odor term aᵢ that moves all of an odor's pairs; drifts over 1–3 s with odor-specific timing; carries the hemibrain bias during the sustained odor.For the drift, a slow per-odor gain: KC-intrinsic mAChR-A, synaptic depression at the bouton, ORN/PN adaptation or slow LN GABA-B upstream. Not APL: in the labmate data the APL effect follows the odor part (r +0.82) but not its drift (r +0.13); APL holds the odor part down at the soma.Each odor's aᵢ vs its total bouton drive (needs the amplitude data behind 7B). With APL silenced, the labmate somata show the odor part released and no link to the drift (exploratory); claw imaging under silencing would place it. Timescale: APL's measured lag is a few hundred ms, so 1–3 s needs a slow receptor or adaptation.
Pair partWhat two odors share beyond their row terms; present at 0.5 s, stable (r 0.92–0.96 across windows), matched by the somata, predicted by either wiring model.One step of PN→KC integration leaking into claw calcium: dendritic depolarization and back-propagation (5), KC→KC synapses (6), set by which boutons the ~14 KCs on a bouton also sample (7). The ester–aldehyde block may add regionalized inhibition (4).A claw-input model: hemibrain sampling matrix applied as W·Wᵀ onto bouton coordinates, no threshold, should beat the KC-output models. Single-KC claw imaging measures the cell-wide share directly. KC-output block removes back-propagation but not dendritic integration.

The loci are drawn for one microglomerulus; APL, MB-C1 and the KC→KC synapses act across the whole calyx. A caveat that cuts the other way: boutons and claws are sub-micron apart, so optical crosstalk makes claws look more like boutons, which works against every claw-specific component here, not for it.
